What the table holds

The table has 206 calling codes used by 245 countries and territories: 2 of one digit, 44 of two digits and 160 of three digits. Most codes belong to one country. 12 are shared: +1 by 25 places, +44 by 4 places, +590 by 3 places, +61 by 3 places, +212 by 2 places, +262 by 2 places, +290 by 2 places, +358 by 2 places, +39 by 2 places, +47 by 2 places, +599 by 2 places and +7 by 2 places. In a shared row, the main country is named first and the digits in brackets are the area code that tells a smaller place apart.

The first digit shows the part of the world

Country codes were handed out by world zone, so the first digit after the + already narrows down where a call comes from.

  • +1: North America and many Caribbean islands, all sharing the single code +1. 1 code on this page.
  • +2: Africa, with a few Atlantic and Indian Ocean islands. 61 codes on this page, starting with +20 Egypt, +211 South Sudan and +212 Morocco.
  • +3: Europe. 34 codes on this page, starting with +30 Greece, +31 Netherlands and +32 Belgium.
  • +4: Europe. 12 codes on this page, starting with +40 Romania, +41 Switzerland and +420 Czechia.
  • +5: Mexico, Central America and South America. 28 codes on this page, starting with +500 Falkland Islands, +501 Belize and +502 Guatemala.
  • +6: South-east Asia and Oceania. 28 codes on this page, starting with +60 Malaysia, +61 Australia and +62 Indonesia.
  • +7: Russia and Kazakhstan, which share +7. 1 code on this page.
  • +8: East Asia, and some international services. 11 codes on this page, starting with +81 Japan, +82 South Korea and +84 Vietnam.
  • +9: West, South and Central Asia. 30 codes on this page, starting with +90 Turkey, +91 India and +92 Pakistan.

How to read an unknown number

An international number starts with + and the country code, which is one, two or three digits long. No code is the beginning of another code, so only one row can match. For example +971 is the United Arab Emirates, not +97 followed by 1, because +97 alone is not a code.

Be careful with missed calls from abroad

A single ring from an unknown foreign number can be a "missed call" trick: the caller hopes you will ring back a number that charges high rates. If you do not know anyone in that country, you do not need to call back.
